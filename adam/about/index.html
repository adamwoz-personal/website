About Adam

Principal engineer building AI and machine-learning systems end to end — agentic LLM pipelines, production classifiers, and the data platforms that feed them.

I build AI and machine-learning systems end to end: agentic LLM pipelines, classical classifiers that have to hold up in production, and the data platforms that feed both. Over 20+ years I designed or implemented 50+ production classifiers and owned a portfolio of roughly 200 for McAfee/Intel Global Threat Intelligence. Most of my hands-on time now goes to modern GenAI — self-hosted LLMs, agentic tool-calling, MCP servers, RAG, and the benchmark harnesses that show whether any of it actually works.

Most recently I was Principal Data Architect at Zimperium, where I led the Data, Edge, and Application Analysis engineering organizations and directed the pipelines feeding mobile threat-detection models — from raw device and app telemetry through feature extraction to classifier output and customer-facing verdicts. I also built an AI-powered regional mobile threat-assessment portal there. Before Zimperium I spent eighteen years at a single team that was acquired four times — CipherTrust to Secure Computing to McAfee to Intel and back out to McAfee — and was promoted from L3 Support through Principal Engineer, Data Architect, and Research Architect along the way.

I work from evidence: reproducible benchmarks, held-out evaluation, and measured routing decisions instead of vendor claims. Security taught me that habit — a classifier that is confidently wrong buries a customer help desk in false-positive tickets. I've also spent a lot of time explaining what the data says: hundreds of press interviews, keynotes and training courses, a principal-engineering committee chair, and service as an FBI escalation contact.

I hold two U.S. patents on machine-learning techniques for network-security telemetry and I'm a Georgia Tech CS graduate. I live in Lilburn, GA.
